1. Survey the job market.  
   - Think about the companies and types of roles you are interested in. Find job postings by those companies and for related roles. Start to understand: 
     1. what roles sound interesting to me and 
     2. what skills and qualities are these job postings asking for. 

2. Craft your resume and LinkedIn.
   - Create a resume (and correspondingly **update LinkedIn)** to highlight your experience related to the types of roles you have identified. 
     - If you will be applying to multiple categories of roles, you might need to craft multiple versions of your resume. 
     - Try to connect with everyone you on LinkedIn know to develop a presence. 

3. Network in your desired area. 
   - Reach out to people in the companies and fields that you are interested in. Initially, ask for informational interviews where you can ask for advice. For people who are ahead of you on the career path you are interested in, understand their career progression and ask for advice about how to make yourself more valuable. For people who work at the companies you are interested in, ask about what kinds of skills and experiences they look for in roles like the ones you are interested in. 

4. **Fill your gaps.**
   - As you learn more about your desired field, companies, and roles, you will identify gaps in your skills. Work on personal projects and/or courses that fill those gaps. 

5. Work on your interviewing skills. 
   - If you are looking for a coding job, you will need to start practicing technical interviewing skills, like coding interviews and systems design interviews. For all kinds of jobs, you will want to practice behavioral interviewing skills. 

6. Continue to network and apply for jobs.
   - Continue a multi-pronged approach to the job search, splitting your time between:
        1. Applying to posted jobs. This is a numbers game, and so it's all about doing a certain volume per day/week.
        2. Continue to network. You are much more likely to get a job via a personal connection. So, this is the most effective way to find a job. 
        3. Filling gaps. Continue building and demonstrating skills via personal projects and courses.
